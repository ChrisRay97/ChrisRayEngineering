Overview

The Keystone Dev Board is a compact, ruggedized microcontroller platform designed from the ground up for industrial sensor interfacing and harsh field environments. Unlike general-purpose development boards, Keystone prioritizes signal integrity, robust I/O protection, and long-term field reliability over breadboard compatibility or low unit cost.

Design goals

The primary design constraint was a board that could be deployed in an enclosure without modification — no jumper wires, no bodge resistors, no rework. Every interface is broken out to a latching industrial connector and every input is protected to at minimum ±24V transient tolerance.

Block diagram — power architecture and I/O routing

Fig. 1 — System block diagram showing power domains and signal paths

Power input accepts 7–36V unregulated DC through a reverse-polarity protected Anderson connector. Three switching regulators generate isolated 5V, 3.3V, and an adjustable analog reference rail. All regulators are rated to 85°C ambient without derating.

Firmware

The board ships with a minimal HAL written in C that exposes all peripherals over a documented serial protocol. A Python wrapper library handles host-side communication for rapid prototyping. Both are MIT licensed and available on GitHub.
